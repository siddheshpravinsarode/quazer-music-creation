Good question—if you start building a music generator without a few basics, you’ll end up guessing instead of designing. You don’t need to become a musician, but you *do* need a small “mental map” of how music works.

Here’s the **minimum music knowledge you should learn before starting your project**.

---

# 1. Notes (Pitch System) — ABSOLUTE BASIC

You must understand:

* What a note is (C, D, E…)
* Octaves (C3, C4, C5)
* MIDI numbers (important for coding)

Example:

* Middle C = 60
* C# = 61
* D = 62

👉 Think: “pitch = number system”

---

# 2. Scales (VERY IMPORTANT for your idea)

This is directly your “scale gatekeeper”.

You should know:

* Major scale (happy sound)
* Minor scale (sad/dark sound)
* Pentatonic scale (safe for AI music)

Example:

* C major: C D E F G A B
* C minor pentatonic: C Eb F G Bb

👉 Why it matters:
Your system literally depends on “allowed notes only”.

---

# 3. Rhythm (TIME in music)

This is where most beginners fail.

You need to understand:

* Beat = pulse
* Tempo = speed (BPM)
* Note lengths:

  * whole note
  * half note
  * quarter note
  * eighth note

Example:

* 120 BPM = 120 beats per minute

👉 In your system:
This becomes your “duration values”

---

# 4. Chords (basic harmony)

You don’t need deep theory, just basics:

* A chord = multiple notes played together
* Common ones:

  * C major = C + E + G
  * A minor = A + C + E

👉 Why it matters:
Markov chains often work better on chords than single notes.

---

# 5. Melody vs Harmony (IMPORTANT CONCEPT)

You must separate:

* Melody = single note line (your Markov output)
* Harmony = background chords (optional layer)

👉 Your system:

* Markov = melody
* L-system = structure
* Optional chords = depth

---

# 6. Musical “Feel” (MOST IMPORTANT FOR AI MUSIC)

This is not theory-heavy, but crucial:

* Stepwise motion = smooth melody
* Big jumps = chaotic music
* Repetition = catchy music

👉 This directly maps to:

* Markov probabilities

Example:

* 70% small jumps → calm music
* 70% big jumps → chaotic music

---

# 7. Basic song structure (L-SYSTEM PART)

Songs are not random—they have structure:

Typical:

* Intro
* Verse
* Build
* Drop / climax
* Outro

👉 Your L-system = generates this structure automatically

---

# 8. MIDI basics (FOR CODING)

You should know:

* MIDI = digital music instructions
* It stores:

  * note
  * duration
  * velocity

👉 This is your final output format.

---

# 9. How all of this maps to YOUR project

Here’s the clean mapping:

| Music Concept | Your System         |
| ------------- | ------------------- |
| Notes         | MIDI numbers        |
| Scales        | Gatekeeper filter   |
| Rhythm        | duration values     |
| Melody        | Markov chain        |
| Structure     | L-system            |
| Emotion       | probability weights |

---

# 10. What you should learn FIRST (order)

Do NOT learn everything at once. Do this order:

### Step 1:

Notes + MIDI numbers

### Step 2:

Scales (major + minor + pentatonic)

### Step 3:

Rhythm basics (beat + note lengths)

### Step 4:

Very basic chords

### Step 5:

Then start coding Markov music

---

# 11. What you DON’T need right now

Skip these for now:

* advanced harmony theory
* classical music notation
* orchestration
* sound design
* deep music history

---

# 12. Simple truth

You are not “learning music”.

You are learning:

> “rules that turn into patterns a machine can generate”
